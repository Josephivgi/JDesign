# Stroke Paint

Paint the picture the way a person paints: lay color down with strokes, build form with overlapping passes, let edges break. Do not describe outlines as coordinate shapes. Use the draw tool's stroke items (points with pressure, color, size, brush) for almost everything. Use fill_area for the flat base wash under each area.

## How to paint

1. Make the layers first, in order, with newLayer: Sky, Sea, Land, Lighthouse, Boat, Details. Put new work on the layer it belongs to.
2. Before any stroke, fill_area a flat base wash for Sky, Sea and Land. No white paper shows anywhere the sky or sea is. Then place the light: the sun or glow is three translucent watercolor passes around it, and a warm band lies along the horizon. Every later shadow points away from it, and the lit side of every object faces it. Decide the sun's side before the lighthouse: a lighthouse lit on the left of a sun on the right is wrong.
3. Block in each big area with broad watercolor strokes, size 60 to 140 px, 3 to 5 passes of different colors. Watercolor, marker and dark airbrush washes multiply: a second pass across the first is visibly darker where they overlap, so build sky and water with 3 or more crossing passes and let the overlaps show as deeper bands. A light wash (a pale cloud or glint) lays on top and lightens instead. Washes fade at their edges, so they never carry the edge alone. Vary the pass length. No pass is a straight line, and no two passes are the same length or angle.
4. Build each object with 2 to 4 tone passes: a lit stroke along the light side, a mid pass across the form, a dark pass on the shadow side. Each pass follows the form: a tower's strokes bend across it like a barrel, a rock's strokes run with its slope, and unequal widths make the lit and shadow sides differ.
5. Add the edge last: a few pencil or pen strokes at 2 to 6 px along the silhouette where it is hard, and none where it dissolves into light. An edge stroke ends at a silhouette corner; it never crosses the inside of the object.
6. Put the strongest contrast at the focus. Keep the light in one direction across all layers.

## Rules

- Strokes are long and curved. Each one has 6 to 20 points. Only pen, pencil and calligraphy change width with pressure (start light, press, lift). Marker, airbrush and watercolor keep their width along the body and end differently at each end (a short blunt start, a long thin tail that breaks into dry-brush streaks), so use them for washes and never for a pressed line. Start a pass where the brush lands and let it trail off: do not plan the two ends as mirror images.
- A watercolor, airbrush or marker stroke never closes into a ring: a ring of wash is a sausage cloud or a stock blob. Build a cloud from several open passes. Each pass differs from the last by at least 40% in length and 15 px in height. Lighter on top, darker and flatter below, with one broken side.
- No short stroke (under 600 px) runs ruler-straight: bend it at least 2.5% of its length (a 200 px stroke bends 5 px), or split it into shorter passes. A long horizon or water pass may run across the canvas, but it still has to bend.
- Never repeat a stroke. Five strokes of the same length, size and angle are refused as a row. Vary each one.
- No dashes, no hatching grids, no rows of short marks, no dotted lines. A texture is many overlapping strokes of different lengths, not repeated marks.
- Mix colors: no pure red, orange, yellow, green, black or white. Lean each color toward its neighbors. Darks are deep, not black.
- Water is long horizontal strokes that taper at their ends, bunched near the front, lighter far away. Reflections are broken strokes, not a mirror.
- Rocks: three overlapping tone passes of different lengths, and a few dark cracks drawn as one curved stroke each. Never a ruler edge.
- Clouds: 4 to 6 passes of clearly different length (one long, one half as long, a short one) that overlap and drift at different heights, so the mass is wider at one end. Lighter on top, a darker flatter pass below, one broken side where it dissolves into the sky. No stock lumps and no stacked slabs. Never two parallel capsules one above the other, and never two passes of the same length.
- Water near the horizon may be long flat passes, but never five of the same length stacked in a row. Vary each one.
- Every object is several strokes, never one primitive. A lighthouse is: a lit pass on the sun's side (about 60% of the tower width), a narrower dark pass on the other side that overlaps it by a few px, a stone base of 2 or 3 short wide dark passes that flare wider than the tower and sit on the rock, a gallery pass under the lantern, and a short warm stroke for the lantern glow. Never one tapered stroke, and never a symmetric cone: the two tower passes differ in width, length and lean.
- The sea is 6 or more passes over the base wash, in three groups: long dark swell passes, each a different length and bent, spaced closer and thicker toward the front; mid passes of the sea's own mid color between them; and under the sun a broken path of pale short passes (3 to 6, uneven gaps, uneven lengths, widest at the horizon end of the path) with dark gaps between them. Far water is lighter and thinner, near water darker and wider.
- Overlap joined parts by a few pixels, so there is no gap between them.

## Checking

After each layer, view_drawing. Look for: a stroke that reads as a line drawn with a ruler, a repeated mark, a flat block with no pass inside it, white paper where a base wash belongs, and a silhouette that is perfectly symmetric. Fix those with another pass, not by deleting the layer.
