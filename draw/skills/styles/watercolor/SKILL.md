# Watercolor

Soft washes that bleed into each other on textured paper. The example image was drawn in JDraw with these steps.

## The look

Watercolor painting, soft wet-on-wet washes, pigment blooms and bleeding edges, visible cold-press paper texture, loose brushwork, light airy palette.

Avoid: Hard outlines, photorealism, 3D render.

## Palette

`#fbf8f1` paper, `#5aa7e0` sky, `#f39a1e` sun, `#f9c74f`, `#5b6db8` far hills, `#2f6f86` near hills, `#5f9e3a` grass, `#2d7a3a` leaves, `#5a3a24` bark

Add the colors you use with set_palette so the person can paint with them too.

## How to draw it in JDraw

- Paper first: canvas background warm off-white (#fbf8f1), never pure white.
- Lay big washes with `watercolor` strokes, size 18 to 26, opacity 0.7 to 0.8. Draw them as many slightly wobbly parallel bands across the area instead of one filled shape; overlaps darken like real pigment.
- Fade each wash toward the paper: mix the color with the paper tone as it moves away from the main subject (lighter sky near the horizon).
- Blooms: scatter soft ellipses (opacity 0.15 to 0.2) along edges where two washes meet.
- Soft forms like trees or clouds are 40 to 70 overlapping ellipses at opacity 0.4 to 0.5, two or three close tones mixed.
- Keep each area on its own named layer (Sky wash, Land, Subject) so washes can be redone.
- No black outlines. If edges need help, a darker watercolor stroke at size 3 to 6, opacity 0.6.
- Light comes from the paper: leave unpainted white where the light hits. Let washes bleed unevenly and add grain by speckling a few small dots.
- Avoid (slop): smooth airbrush gradients, oversaturated color, even washes with no white left, a centered symmetrical subject, hard perfect ellipses for trees.

## Check

Call view_drawing when you are done and compare with the example: does it read as watercolor at a glance? Fix what does not.
